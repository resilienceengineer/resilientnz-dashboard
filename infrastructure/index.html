05 Investment assumptions

Shared across sectors. All money is in constant NZ$ millions. Capital is paid now; annual costs and benefits start in year 1.

How this screening model works

Costs = capital + discounted annual maintenance. Benefits = discounted annual avoided damage, service, environmental and community benefits. BCR = benefits ÷ costs. NPV = benefits − costs.

Economic score = 100 × BCR ÷ (1 + BCR). Other dimension scores and disruption reductions are illustrative assumptions. The ranking is the weighted average of four dimension scores.

Each sector compares packages against a no-investment baseline. Road pathways specify treatment quantities, delivery steps and review triggers. Costs cover the stated package at year 0; later trigger-led changes need separate costing. Different pathways address different hazards and residual risks.

Road carbon = construction + annual additional maintenance × years − annual avoided detour / repair emissions × years, scaled by packages. Values are illustrative tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, with no physical discounting. Carbon is shown separately from B/C and the illustrative environmental score. Routine traffic, induced demand, end of life and sequestration are excluded.

No escalation, residual value or replacement cycles are included. Horizon should not exceed the intervention's useful life without revised inputs.

Location, hazard layers and sea-level scenarios provide map context; they do not calibrate costs or rankings. An asset inventory, fragility model and outage analysis are needed for that connection.

Benefit categories must be independently valued to avoid double counting. MCDA dimensions may overlap; the score is a preference index. Shortlisting across sectors does not model dependencies or shared benefits.

Road intervention context: NZTA resilience treatments. Carbon context: NZTA infrastructure carbon research. All quantities and numerical values in this demo are invented for illustration.
